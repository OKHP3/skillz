# Executive decision: order email automation and coordinator staffing

## Recommendation

**Do not approve removal of all four junior coordinator positions next month.** The supplied dry run does not establish reliable order handling, sufficient exception coverage, or four positions’ worth of removable work. Keep current staffing while management evaluates assisted triage through a bounded, read-only replay of historical work.

Treat staffing, mentoring, and customer quote authority as separate decisions. Do not adopt eight apprentices per senior mentor or enable automatic customer quotes on the evidence supplied. Both require their own capacity or authority evidence before approval.

This recommendation uses only the supplied fictional scenario. No employee records, operational systems, or external sources were examined. The proposed evaluation below has not been run.

The central distinction is between reducing time on one task and removing entire roles. Even a verified extraction improvement would leave questions about review, correction, exceptions, other coordinator work, peak coverage, and the work transferred to senior staff.

## Evidence needed before a workforce decision

**Service quality and exception handling.** Obtain a representative view of message types and workloads, including incomplete orders, ambiguous requests, unusual terms, and cases requiring escalation. Establish which fields and decisions are critical, what errors matter most, who resolves exceptions, and how failures are detected. The current scenario supplies none of these distributions or controls.

**Whole-process labor and capacity.** Measure current work from receipt through a usable, approved outcome, including corrections, follow-up, escalation, and work outside email extraction. Evaluate normal demand, busy periods, absence cover, and minimum necessary supervision. Identify whether released hours are contiguous and schedulable enough to change staffing, or instead improve service and absorb demand.

**Development and mentoring.** Verify the manager’s statement using aggregate promotion history over a defined period: cohort size, moves into account ownership, time to progression, retention, and available alternatives. Separately establish mentors’ available hours, their own account workload, apprentice support needs, and escalation burden. Do not describe the existing roles as a proven talent pipeline without that evidence, or assume a replacement apprenticeship model would preserve it.

**Quote authority.** Document who can approve customer-facing quotations, the limits of that authority, relevant pricing and terms rules, and escalation ownership. A correct extraction does not itself authorize a commercial commitment. Any later quote automation needs an explicitly approved scope and a reliable hold-and-review path for cases outside it.

## Next experiment: read-only historical replay

The next decision should be whether an assisted triage approach merits a controlled operational trial. A historical replay can provide useful evidence without changing live orders, sending quotes, or making employee decisions. It cannot, by itself, prove that four positions can be removed.

1. **Define outcomes and decision rules first.** Agree the tasks being evaluated, error severity categories, acceptable service performance, and the conditions for stopping or narrowing the proposal. Set thresholds before inspecting results. The supplied information is insufficient to choose defensible numerical thresholds today.

2. **Build a representative, authorized sample.** Use de-identified historical messages and available case outcomes from a defined period, covering ordinary work and busy conditions. Supplement the representative sample with a separately reported challenge set of rare or difficult cases. Include the existing 30 messages only if their provenance and selection are understood. Determine sample size from observed variation and the precision required for consequential error rates; do not treat 30 as automatically sufficient.

3. **Establish a reference outcome.** Have qualified reviewers identify required fields, intended routing, necessary escalation, and whether a quote would need approval. Resolve disagreements before judging automation. Report ambiguous cases explicitly rather than forcing a false reference answer. Do not turn reviewer observations into named employee ratings.

4. **Replay both workflows.** Compare the current process with extraction plus human review and exception handling on the same or appropriately matched cases. Count all human effort, not just extraction time. Keep proposed quote outputs within the evaluation and label their status; no quote is sent to a customer. Record which cases the system should decline to resolve automatically.

5. **Evaluate mentoring separately.** Use aggregate work observations or simulations to estimate support time at different apprentice loads. Include routine coaching, interruptions, escalation, preparation, and mentors’ existing duties. Treat eight-to-one as one candidate scenario. Do not assign that load to live teams on the strength of this report.

6. **Review results before changing operations.** Decide whether to reject the approach, narrow it to lower-risk message types, improve it and repeat the affected checks, or propose a controlled live trial. Any operational trial, authority change, or workforce action requires a separately approved plan. The current authorization covers read-only aggregate analysis.

## Measures and how to interpret them

| Measure | What to collect | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Extraction quality | Correctness and completeness by field; proportion of cases with all critical fields correct; severity of errors. | A high overall field score can conceal consequential errors in particular fields. |
| Triage quality | Correct routing; missed escalations; unnecessary escalations; correct handling of ambiguous cases. | Speed is useful only if work reaches the right next step. |
| Exceptions | Frequency, categories, resolution time, unresolved cases, and reviewer disagreement. | Reveals the work that remains human-dependent and its variability. |
| Human effort | Baseline effort versus assisted effort, including review, correction, follow-up, and senior support. | Tests whether the process releases usable capacity or shifts work elsewhere. |
| Service performance | Completion time distribution, backlog, rework, and performance during busy periods. | Average extraction time does not show whether service is sustainable. |
| Quote controls | Cases requiring approval; cases incorrectly classified as within authority; unsupported proposed prices or terms. | Tests whether a future quote workflow can reliably respect its approved boundaries. No external quote is issued in the replay. |
| Mentoring capacity | Support hours per apprentice, variation by experience and case complexity, escalation demand, and mentor capacity remaining for existing duties. | Supplies evidence for a feasible ratio without assuming eight-to-one is safe or productive. |
| Development pathway | Aggregate progression rates, time to progression, retention, and cohort definitions. | Verifies the reported path into account ownership and informs alternatives. |
| Economics and staffing | Net labor hours released, operating costs, realistic roster scenarios, coverage constraints, and sensitivity to demand or error rates. | Separates productivity improvements from realizable payroll savings. |

Report denominators, sample coverage, uncertainty, and consequential failures alongside averages. A short test with no observed failure does not establish that a failure cannot occur.

For capacity analysis, use this accounting identity:

**Net human hours released = baseline human effort − all human effort required by the assisted process.**

Include review, exception resolution, correction, oversight, and any additional mentoring in the assisted-process total. Avoid double-counting the same work. Convert released hours into possible staffing scenarios only after accounting for demand variability, working schedules, absence coverage, other duties, and minimum coverage. Payroll savings require an actual feasible staffing change; a fractional reduction in task time is not that change.

## Executive decision gates

- **Assisted triage:** Consider a limited operational trial only after representative evidence meets agreed quality and service criteria, with clear escalation ownership and measured review effort.
- **Mentoring model:** Choose a ratio only after observed support demand fits available mentor time without compromising existing responsibilities. Retain uncertainty where evidence is sparse.
- **Automatic quotes:** Hold until authority, approval boundaries, exception handling, and outcome quality are documented and approved. Read-only evaluation confers no authority to send quotes.
- **Staffing:** Reconsider only after sustained operational evidence supports a feasible staffing model and the implications for service, development, and workload have been reviewed. The supplied evidence does not support removing four positions next month.

**Next action for management:** designate an evaluation owner and approve the scope, data access, review capacity, and success criteria for the historical replay. The immediate deliverable is an aggregate evidence report and a set of staffing scenarios with explicit assumptions. No named ratings, age-based selections, customer communications, or external actions are part of this analysis.
