# Read the résumé like the person deciding whom to interview

**First 30 seconds.** Read the top third, then look away. Name the problem this candidate can handle, one piece of proof, and the first question you would ask. If the answer is just an adjective or a list of tools, move a decision or result into the opening. Read the role history next. Can you find where and when that evidence came from?

**Evidence pass.** For each number, title, credential, direct-report count, spend figure and shipped or adopted claim, find the supporting fact in the input. Check that a collaborative result gives the candidate and the team appropriate credit. Remove sample content and placeholders. Check every contact field and the actual destination behind any retained portfolio link.

**File pass.** For DOCX or PDF, reopen the final artifact in its target reader if available. Check the rendered page count, the top third, headings at page boundaries, complete bullets, readable type and link behavior. Extract or copy selectable text from the delivered file and read it in order from name through the last line. Watch for contact fields that disappear, interleaved columns, scrambled dates and duplicated headers. If both formats are requested, verify both files contain the same intended résumé. Never describe a check as passed unless it was performed.

**Delivery.** Lead with the résumé text or the requested files. If the environment cannot generate or verify the required file, identify the limitation and provide the best usable draft. Keep any private verification question outside the résumé.

**Further reading.** For an outside editorial check on specificity, results, reverse chronology and PDF layout, see the quick tips from Harvard’s Mignone Center for Career Success. [Harvard College Guide to Creating a Strong Resume](https://careerservices.fas.harvard.edu/resources/create-a-strong-resume/)
