# Worked edit for a career change

This fictional example shows how to make adjacent experience legible when the candidate has no metric or matching title.

**Opening.** A commercial laundry needs a Supplier Claims Assistant who can document damaged deliveries, follow up with suppliers and reconcile credit notes.

**Candidate’s account.** “I was a breakfast cook. Deliveries came in during service. When a carton was damaged, I photographed it, wrote the delivery-note number on the kitchen copy and emailed the supplier. The owner matched credit notes to invoices. I never saw the accounts.”

**Weak draft.** “Results-oriented professional with supplier relationship management and accounts payable experience.”

**Two lines the candidate can use**

> **Breakfast Cook | Copper Ladle Canteen | 2021 to 2024**\
> Documented damaged deliveries during breakfast service. Attached photos and delivery-note numbers to supplier emails so the owner had a record for credit-note reconciliation.

**Why it works.** The photos and delivery-note numbers are evidence of claims work even without a savings figure. The last phrase identifies the owner’s role accurately. The candidate could answer “What did you send the supplier?” from firsthand experience. If asked about reconciling credits, they can describe the handoff. The fictional candidate confirmed the employer, title and dates for this example.
